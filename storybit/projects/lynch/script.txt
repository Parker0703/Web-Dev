In 2003,
America watched a hero get rescued live from an Iraqi hospital…
and almost none of it was true.
Her name was Jessica Lynch.
She was 19 years old, a supply clerk from West Virginia.
March 23rd, 2003.
Her convoy took a wrong turn into the city of Nasiriyah…
and drove straight into an ambush.
Eleven American soldiers were killed.
Jessica was pulled from a wrecked Humvee with a broken spine, a broken arm, and a shattered leg.
She was taken prisoner.
Nine days later, Special Forces stormed the hospital where she was being held.
Night-vision cameras rolled as they carried her out on a stretcher.
And by the next morning,
America had a legend.
Newspapers said she fought until she ran out of ammunition.
That she was shot and stabbed.
That she went down fighting.
Movie deals. Magazine covers.
A hero for a nation at war.
But here's where it gets strange.
Jessica never fired a single shot.
Her rifle jammed.
She wasn't shot.
She wasn't stabbed.
Her injuries came from the crash.
And the hospital?
The Iraqi soldiers had already left it.
The doctors said they'd given her their own blood, and had even tried to drive her back to the Americans in an ambulance…
but U.S. troops opened fire on it at a checkpoint.
When the rescue team stormed in,
there was no one left to fight.
So where did the action-movie version come from?
Nobody ever fully explained it.
And the soldier who actually did fight back that day,
her best friend, Lori Piestewa,
was barely mentioned.
She died of her wounds,
the first Native American woman killed in combat for the U.S. military.
Four years later, Jessica stood in front of Congress and said it herself:
"I'm still confused as to why they chose to lie."
She said she wasn't a hero.
She was a survivor.
And the real heroes were the ones who didn't come home.
