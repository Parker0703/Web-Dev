In 1976, America sent B-52 bombers, gunships and hundreds of troops to the most dangerous border on Earth…
to cut down one tree.
Because three days earlier,
that tree got two American soldiers killed.
It stood inside the Joint Security Area,
the one spot in the Korean DMZ where North and South Korean guards stand face to face.
A hundred-foot poplar was blocking the view between two UN checkpoints.
So on August 18th, a small crew went out to trim it.
North Korean soldiers showed up and ordered them to stop.
The Americans kept working.
Then a North Korean officer gave a signal…
and his men grabbed the crew's own axes.
In less than a minute,
Captain Arthur Bonifas and Lieutenant Mark Barrett were dead.
America was furious.
But instead of starting a war,
it planned the most extreme gardening job in history.
On August 21st, a convoy rolled in.
Engineers with chainsaws.
Special forces ready to fight.
Twenty-seven helicopters overhead.
B-52s flying in from Guam.
An aircraft carrier waiting off the coast.
They called it
Operation Paul Bunyan.
Right in front of the North Korean guards, the chainsaws started.
Nobody fired a shot.
Forty-two minutes later, the tree was gone.
And for the first time ever,
North Korea's leader sent a message of regret.
Two countries almost went back to war…
over a tree.
