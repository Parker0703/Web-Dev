Scientists just found a virus hiding inside a 1,000-year-old book.
Give me 60 seconds, and I'll show you how it got there.
Medieval books weren't made from paper. They were made from animal skin.
So researchers tested the pages from old manuscripts in Britain, France and Austria.
The pages looked perfectly clean. No spots. No damage.
But the DNA told a different story.
It was sheeppox. A deadly disease of livestock.
Don't panic. It's a livestock virus, not a human one.
So why was it inside a book?
Maybe the animal was sick when it died. Or the virus landed on the skin while the parchment was made.
Researchers aren't sure which.
But here's the twist. Those pages let them rebuild the virus's family tree.
It goes back about 3,500 years. The oldest traces come from sheep teeth on the Eurasian steppe.
So how did a virus end up in a book? Because the book is the animal.
Every page was once a living creature… and it never stopped telling its story.
