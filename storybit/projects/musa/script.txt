One king was so rich that he accidentally made gold cheaper in another country.
And people were still talking about it years later.
In 1324, Mansa Musa, ruler of the Mali Empire, traveled to Mecca.
But his journey through Cairo turned into a legendary display of wealth.
His caravan reportedly included thousands of people and camels carrying enormous amounts of gold.
And when he arrived in Cairo, he started giving gold away.
To officials. Merchants. Religious leaders. Almost everyone he met.
But he gave away so much that he flooded the city's gold market.
Suddenly, gold became less valuable.
And this wasn't just some temporary rumor.
A historian named Al-Umari later recorded that gold prices in Egypt had fallen after Musa's visit and remained depressed for years.
Think about that.
Most kings tried to conquer cities to gain their wealth.
Mansa Musa simply walked into one of the richest cities in the world... and gave away so much gold that its value dropped.
He didn't conquer Cairo.
He accidentally messed with its economy just by spending money.
