In 1932, Australia sent soldiers armed with machine guns to fight 20,000 giant birds.
And somehow, the birds won.
After World War I, thousands of Australian veterans had settled on farmland in Western Australia.
But they had a serious problem: emus.
Every year, these giant flightless birds migrated through farmland, destroying crops and damaging fences.
Frustrated farmers begged the government for help.
So the government sent in the military.
Two soldiers. Machine guns. Thousands of rounds of ammunition.
What could possibly go wrong?
On November 2, the soldiers spotted a massive group of emus and opened fire.
But the birds scattered in every direction.
They were surprisingly fast, difficult to hit, and seemed completely impossible to control.
The soldiers even mounted a machine gun on a truck, hoping to chase them down.
But the rough terrain made aiming almost impossible.
And the emus just kept running.
After several failed attempts, the military operation was called off.
The soldiers had fired thousands of rounds, but the emus were still everywhere.
And that's how Australia became famous for fighting a war against birds... and losing.
